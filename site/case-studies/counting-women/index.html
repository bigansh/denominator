Case study Not an index

India records the fewest rapes and asks the fewest questions.

England and Wales records 55.9 times more rape per head of population than India. Correcting for reporting does not close the gap, and the gap still says nothing about safety. This is a study of what the statistical system can see, and of the one ranking the evidence actually supports.

India's denominators are derived from NCRB's own published rates — 441,534 crimes against women at 64.6 per lakh women implies 683.5m women; 5.89m cognizable cases at 418.9 per lakh implies 1,406m people. The E&W denominator is ONS mid-2023. The ratio is 55.9×. The two numbers are not the same offence and the comparison is not interpretable in either direction. The section below sets out why.

What this page does not claim
  • No statement of the form "India is safer or less safe than X." Not one, anywhere below.
  • No harassment rate for India. No national instrument measures it.
  • No reporting rate for India. The figure of roughly 1% is inferred from help-seeking, never measured.
  • No current level of partner violence for India. NFHS-6 is discontinuous and its full report is still pending.
  • No trend. Two rounds with a break between them is not a trend.
  • Nothing about China. No current public instrument was identified, and that absence is itself a result.

The headline number

Four reasons the 55.9× gap is not a fact about the world

Each of these breaks the comparison on its own. Together they make the recorded rape rate uninterpretable between these two jurisdictions.

Legal truncation. Section 63 of the BNS retains the marital rape exception. In England and Wales, 43.2% of victims of rape or assault by penetration were victimised by a partner or ex-partner, rising to 44.5% among females. India's count structurally cannot contain the category that supplies close to half of England's. Meanwhile NCRB records 96.8% of rape accused in 2024 as known to the survivor: the one relationship carved out is the most common one.

Counting rules. Home Office rules changed in July 2016 to record rape per incident. India records one case per FIR. Repeat victimisation multiplies one side and not the other.

Exposure. Per-capita divides by all women, including those who are not in public. NFHS-5 finds 42% of women are allowed to go alone to all three of market, health facility and outside the village. A rate computed over a partly confined population is not comparable to one computed over a population that is not.

Instrument. CSEW is a victimisation survey with a self-completion module. NFHS is a household interview with a married-women-only spousal module administered to a subsample. Indirect elicitation raises measured partner violence by up to 39% in Nigeria and 100% in Rwanda against direct face-to-face questioning.

Reported against ourselves: the reporting correction does not close the gap. Doubling India's reporting rate would double India's recorded number, not leave it unchanged. But the residual 4.2× is not evidence that England is more dangerous, because the definitional and counting defects above remain uncorrected and both run the same way. The conclusion is that the indicator does not work, not that one country wins.

The inversion

The recorded rate inverts prevalence inside one legal system

One penal code, one police manual, one survey instrument. If the comparison fails here, it carries nothing across a border.

Kerala's recorded rate of crime against women runs at roughly twice Bihar's, and its recorded rate for cruelty by a husband at almost four times Bihar's. NFHS-5 reports the opposite ordering in measured prevalence. Kerala also reports among the lowest sexual violence prevalence in the country, at 1%, alongside the lowest freedom of movement in India, at 15%.

A recorded crime rate is a measure of the propensity to report and the willingness to register. Where reporting is easier, the number is higher. That is the whole finding, and it is visible without leaving India.

What just happened to India's own numbers

Both of India's instruments broke in the same reporting year

The survey and the police series each moved sharply, and in each case the movement is attributable to the instrument rather than to the world. One break would be a caveat. Two, in one year, is the reason this page is not an index.

A 30-point fall in four years has no precedent in any country's violence series. Zero urban respondents in Karnataka disclosed childhood sexual abuse. That is not a finding about Karnataka; it is a finding about the interview.

Researchers comparing the two rounds report that the question structure and routing logic were identical, which removes the benign explanation. If the instrument did not change and the number halved, what changed is the condition of disclosure. Fieldworker effects account for around 32% of total variation in measured partner violence.

The fact sheet carries 101 indicators against NFHS-5's 131. Among those dropped: sex ratio at birth, all three child mortality rates, all seven anaemia breakdowns. The sheets were reportedly ready for roughly a year before release, which came after the 2024 general election and the April 2026 state elections. The ministry describes the publication as interim.

WHO's 2023 prevalence estimates draw on data published to November 2024, so they still rest on NFHS-5. India's global anchor and India's own newest survey now disagree, and every population-weighted number built on WHO inherits that.

The Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita replaced the Indian Penal Code during 2024. The transition renumbered offences and changed some definitions and scope, and NCRB itself advises caution in comparing 2024 with earlier years. The headline that crime fell 6% is therefore not a finding about crime. Rape recorded fell 7.8% in the same year that the survey instrument recorded a national fall in partner violence and a 30-point fall in one state. Neither movement can be read as progress, and the two cannot be used to corroborate each other.

Composition, 2024: cruelty by husband or relatives 120,227 (27.2%), kidnapping and abduction 67,829 (15.4%), POCSO 67,809 (15.4%), assault with intent to outrage modesty 48,303 (10.9%), rape 29,536 (6.7%). Cybercrime rose 17.9%. Note that the widely reported "rape = 10.4%" figure uses a different denominator — offences against women and children, 284,530 cases — not crimes against women.

Voyeurism under BNS 77 requires a "private act", so photographing a woman's body on a train is not covered. India has no dedicated exhibitionism offence; it falls under obscene acts and is not separately tabulated. Frequency ranking from Safecity's crowdsourced report categories: in NCR, commenting exceeds a quarter of reports, then touching and groping at 16%, catcalls at 14%, ogling and staring at 14%. In cities outside the metros, touching and groping is the single largest category at over a fifth. Where an offence code does exist, the count is small enough to make the point on its own: Delhi, a city of over twenty million, registered 316 cases of sexual harassment and 279 of assault with intent to disrobe in 2024 — both the highest of any of the 19 metropolitan cities.

The price

Delhi women pay double the annual tuition for a safer route, and no statistic records it

Borker, World Bank Policy Research Working Paper 9731. 4,000 students across eight Delhi colleges, every feasible route mapped, merged with Safetipin and Safecity route-level safety data.

89% of the women had faced harassment while travelling; 63% unwanted staring. Women accept a college in the bottom half of the quality distribution over one in the top quintile to travel a safer route. The premium is roughly double Delhi University's average annual tuition and about 7% of Delhi per-capita income. Not one rupee of it appears in any crime statistic. Borker names the obvious downstream: it is a candidate explanation for India's female labour force participation.

The one clean comparison

Identical method, two jurisdictions, 13.8 against 7.1

India13.8%
UK + USA7.1%

Share of tweets mentioning women politicians rated problematic or abusive. Amnesty Troll Patrol: 114,716 tweets to 95 Indian women politicians, March–May 2019; 778 women in the UK and USA, 2017. Crowdsourced decoding plus machine learning, same protocol both times.

This is the only like-for-like cross-national measurement in the entire case study, and it is not a government statistic. One in five of the abusive Indian tweets was sexist or misogynistic. Muslim women politicians received 94.1% more religious slurs; women from marginalised castes 59% more casteist slurs.

The recording gap alongside it: those 95 women alone attract on the order of 10,000 abusive mentions a day. India's entire national record of cybercrime with a sexual-exploitation motive is 4,199 cases for 2023. The units differ, so read it as an order of magnitude rather than a ratio. It still holds.

What India has, and what it does not

Ten of the twelve verified members run a dedicated violence-against-women survey. India does not. Its only population-based measure of violence against women is a module inside a health survey, administered to ever-married women, about their husbands. There is no national crime victimisation survey; the ORF and EPW pieces arguing for one are how you know it does not exist.

Korea's survey is statutory, mandated by the Sexual Violence Prevention and Victims Protection Act, and Korea separately compiles Women's Violence Statistics every three years across Justice, the Supreme Prosecutors' Office and the National Police Agency. Mexico's ENDIREH measures violence in five settings including the community, at 110,127 completed interviews, and added image-based abuse in 2021.

What would close it

One questionnaire module on a survey already in the field

NFHS-6 reached 716,397 women. EU-GBV reached 114,023 across 27 countries and is the only harmonised multi-country instrument in existence. Adding a past-12-month public-space harassment module by act type — comments, following, staring, groping, filming, exposure — to the next NFHS round, using EU-GBV wording, would make India the first non-EU country with a comparable measure of the harms that determine whether women occupy public space.

Three further things would move India's capacity score without new fieldwork: publish the victim reporting rate as an official statistic, as Korea does; disaggregate the existing sexual violence question by act; and separate the violence module from the health survey so that unmarried women are in scope.

Of 25 benchmark rows in this study

Comparable, partially comparable, not comparable, and single-country rows with no comparator. Six rows out of twenty-five support a cross-national statement. That proportion, not any number inside it, is the finding of this page.
